## Initial assessment (unconfirmed, no logs yet)

1. **It is not clear what "goes offline" means.** It could be the gateway losing its cellular link to ACS,
   or the remotes dropping off the Dust mesh. Either one shows up in ACS as a missed reading.
2. **Most likely cause: the same failure as `../2026-10-19_socket-errors/`.**
   If the cellular link at the site is weak or flaky, the gateway hits a chain of errors:
   `Sock Recv ERROR` → `Too many failed sockets` → `SW Reseting... Type=10`.
   Each of those app restarts takes down the Dust network, so the remotes drop and have to power up and rejoin again.
   That matches "connects, then quickly goes offline", and both units behaving the same way.
   It also explains why the unit worked at the branch, where coverage is better.
   `../2026-11-11_reboot_no_mote_conn/` turned out to be this exact failure.
3. **If the uplink is fine, look at the mesh.** Possible causes are the repeater path to the remotes in the classified area,
   or RF interference. In that case the gateway would stay connected to ACS while individual remotes go missing.
4. **The ACS list suggests there are two separate problems** (see below):
   - 17 motes went quiet together on 11/17. That matches the gateway or uplink dropping out.
   - 3 motes have been silent since 09/30. That looks like a mesh or placement problem.

## The current outage is on the cellular side, not the mesh (2026-11-18 1:35 PM)

At about 1:25 PM `GetMessages` was texted to 577-612-0327. By 1:33 PM the gateway still hadn't polled: all 7 queued records
(1810255–1810261, including an RSSI script) were still waiting.

**Conclusion:** the gateway itself can't be reached over cellular. If the problem were only the mesh or the motes,
the gateway would still answer `GetMessages` and take its queued jobs. It doesn't, so the uplink is down.
The site-wide missed readings follow from that: with no uplink, mote readings can't reach ACS.
This rules out the mesh-only explanation (initial assessment item 3) for the current outage.
The three motes silent since 09/30 may still be a separate mesh problem.

**Coverage, or the site's antenna?** Both remain possible:
- **Cellular coverage at the site:** AT&T LTE Cat-M1 service at East Dubuque, a tower outage, or band issues.
- **The site's antenna setup:** the external antenna, its cable or a connector. **The gateway swap didn't change the antenna.**
  Both gateways used the same external antenna at Location 1, so it is the one thing they shared.
  "Works at the branch, fails at the site" fits a faulty antenna path just as well as poor coverage.

The signal log leans slightly toward the antenna path. The signal never fades to a weak level; it switches between
strong (about −63 dBm) and 0, for up to 26 hours at a time. Patchy coverage would usually also show weak readings in between.
But a carrier or tower outage could look the same.

**What a 0 in the signal log means:** it does not mean a very weak signal. The log records oFono's `Strength`,
and oFono gives no strength value when the modem isn't registered on a network; the logging script then records 0.
So every 0 means **the modem had lost network registration**. It doesn't mean a measured weak signal.
The log contains no weak values at all (nothing between 0 and 70), only strong-and-registered or not registered.

**Possible causes of losing registration:**
- **Antenna path:** something blocking or breaking the external antenna, its cable or a connector (including water getting in)
- **Carrier or tower:** an AT&T LTE-M outage, or the local tower dropping Cat-M1 service
- **Modem or SIM locking up**

**The reboot argues against a modem lock-up.** The unit rebooted at about 11/13 03:30, in the middle of the
16-hour outage (11/13 00:00–15:45), but registration didn't come back until 16:00, about 12 hours later.
A hung modem or SIM usually recovers when the unit reboots. This one didn't, which points to a cause outside the gateway:
the antenna path or the carrier. That fits support's finding that the hardware works at the branch.

**Source note:** these values come from the gateway's own 15-minute signal log on its REST results channel (11/03–11/15).
They don't come from `examples/rssi.sh`, which hasn't run yet because the unit is offline.

**On-site tests to tell them apart:**
1. **Swap the antenna path:** try a different external antenna and cable, or the gateway's own antenna.
   If the drops stop, the antenna path was the cause.
2. **Check coverage with an AT&T phone at Location 1** during a drop.
   Also look up AT&T outage reports and LTE-M coverage for East Dubuque.
3. **Record the time of each drop**, so it can be matched against the gateway's signal log and the ACS missed readings.

When the gateway next takes its queued jobs (watched every 30 minutes), that time shows when the uplink came back.

**Update 11/18 2:51 PM:** between 2:21 and 2:51 PM the gateway took 6 of its 7 queued records (1810255–1810260),
so the uplink was up at least briefly. That's about 1.5 hours after the 1:25 PM `GetMessages` text.
By 3:17 PM it still hadn't posted any results, and the last record (1810261) was still queued.
That fits a short connection window that dropped again before the jobs' results could be posted (the "connects, then drops" symptom).
The results should be saved on the unit; a `probe` can make it resend them the next time it connects.

**Update 11/19 5:21 AM:** the gateway polled twice more. Between 3:22 and 3:51 PM on 11/18 it took the last record of our jobs (1810261).
Between 4:51 and 5:21 AM on 11/19 it took record 1810267, an immediate `reboot` queued by another operator, not part of our jobs.
So it reaches the REST service roughly every 12 hours, but **it has still posted no results for any of the four jobs**,
and its results channel has stayed at 1,892 records since 11/17. Reading the queue works; posting results apparently doesn't,
or the connection drops before the post. The reboot result itself won't be posted, because the unit restarts before it can post.
`examples/rssi.sh` (job `6fcd9064…`) will then report the signal as CSQ and dBm at that moment.
